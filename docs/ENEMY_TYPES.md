🛡️ Enemy Types in Bolo (1982)
In Bolo, enemy tanks emerge continuously from six bases scattered through the maze. As the level increases, the kinds of enemy tanks change — both in how they move and how threatening they are.
🔹 1. Primitive Drone Tanks
Where: Early levels
Behavior: Slow, simple movement — they often wander or patrol randomly rather than actively chase the player.
Threat Level: Relatively low; they’re primarily a nuisance and good for early practice.
Notes: These are the most numerous early enemies and give way to tougher models as levels rise.
🔹 2. Torpedo (Faster) Tanks
Where: Mid-range levels
Behavior: Move faster than drones and have improved targeting.
Threat Level: Moderate — they close distance quicker and force you to dodge more often.
Notes: They still don’t use advanced pathfinding but can catch careless players.
🔹 3. Hunter/Killer Tanks
Where: Higher difficulty levels
Behavior: More aggressive and quicker. They actively pursue the player rather than roaming.
Threat Level: High — A significant step up, especially in maze chokepoints.
Notes: They make routing choices that put pressure on your position; learning turret handling becomes important against them.
🔹 4. Intelligent Assassin Tanks
Where: Top difficulty play
Behavior: Fastest and smartest enemies. They attempt to track and intercept your tank by choosing efficient routes through the maze.
Threat Level: Very high — They’ll aggressively cut you off and shoot, often faster than you can react.
Notes: These are the “boss-style” enemies of Bolo — once they appear, survival depends on careful movement and turret use.
🎯 General Enemy Behavior Notes
All enemy tanks fire shells, which can destroy you on contact — you lose a turn if hit.
Enemy tanks don’t just come from one base; all six supply enemies continuously until each base is destroyed.
Some tanks merely wander until they spot you, while others use chase logic to hunt you down more strategically.
📈 Difficulty Progression
Level selection (1–9) influences:
Which tank types appear (higher levels introduce smarter, faster enemies earlier).
Maze density (more walls make navigation and escape harder).

In Bolo (1982) the level number (1–9) doesn’t just change the enemy types. It also influences several other aspects of gameplay:
### 🎮 What the Level (1–9) Affects
#### Enemy Variety & Behavior
Higher levels introduce more advanced enemies (faster, smarter, more aggressive), as you already noted.
#### Enemy Speed & Challenge
As levels go up, enemy tanks move faster and can chase you more aggressively. Players from that era report that at high levels enemies can become very fast and hard to escape.
#### Player Strategy Demand
Because enemies get better at pursuing you, higher levels effectively require more turret control and sharper movement — not a formal game mechanic change, but a big gameplay impact.
### 🧱 Related But Separate: Density (1–5)
Importantly, Bolo also asks for a density setting (1–5) when you start the game, and this isn’t the same as the level number:
- Density controls how many walls are in the maze — a higher density means a more cramped maze.
- This affects navigation, line of sight, and how easy or hard it is to escape or fight enemies, but does not change enemy types or AI.
### 🧠 Summary
Game Option | What It Changes
Level (1–9) | Enemy types, enemy speed & aggressiveness
Density (1–5) | Maze layout complexity (walls and corridors)

So the difficulty setting in Bolo is not just enemy variety — it also changes how intense encounters feel because enemies get faster and pursue more effectively, and you can choose a separate maze difficulty that affects movement and tactical options.
